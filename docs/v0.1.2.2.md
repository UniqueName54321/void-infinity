# v0.1.2.2

## Human Play

Human Play now uses seeded smooth noise instead of repeating sinusoidal drift.
Standard reaches have different curved paths, small overshoots and corrections,
varied aim points, idle hand movement, and variable keypress lengths. Slider
following and spinning also vary. Mania adds finger-specific timing and release
variation; taiko varies hands and swell rhythm; catch adds aim drift and relaxed
reach corrections. Shared timing variation keeps replay frames ordered.

The seed makes the same settings reproducible. These are procedural approximations,
not a model trained or calibrated on human replay data. Human Play remains autoplay,
with its mandatory 95%-opacity corner badge and existing automation restrictions.
Turning Human Play off retains normal autoplay behavior.

## Multitap circles (standard)

Right-click a normal circle and choose **Configure multitap**. Set:

- **Number of taps:** 1–1024. A count of 1 restores a normal circle.
- **Time signature:** 1–32 beats per bar and a power-of-two beat unit from 1 to 64.
  The numerator initially follows the map's signature; changing the denominator
  changes the beat unit. Changing only the numerator does not change tap spacing.
- **Taps per beat:** 1–64. In 4/4, four taps per beat gives a quarter-beat stream.

For example, at 120 BPM, 4/4 with four taps per beat means one tap every 125 ms.
3/8 with two taps per beat also means 125 ms; its bar grouping differs. Tempo
changes during the object are respected, including changes between two taps.
This setting belongs to the object and does not rewrite the map's timing points.

Accept applies one undoable change; Cancel or invalid values leave the map alone.
Each tap has its own timing judgement, accuracy, combo contribution, and hitsound.
A remaining-taps counter appears above the circle. Holding a key does not count
as repeated taps. Autoplay, including Human Play, generates the required inputs.

Multitaps use native `.vib` beatmaps and `.vibz` archives. The legacy `.osu` carrier
only represents the first circle; it cannot preserve this feature. Multitaps are
currently standard-only; conversion to other modes is rejected explicitly.

## Converting a stacked stream

Select two or more normal circles at exactly the same authored position, then
right-click and choose **Convert stacked stream to multitap**. The option appears
when all selected notes fit one supported beat subdivision (1–64 taps per quarter
note), with no later new-combo boundaries. The first circle's combo settings
and each circle's hitsounds are retained. Unselected objects are untouched.

Conversion refuses simultaneous notes, irregular spacing, different positions,
and incompatible combo boundaries instead of silently changing the pattern.
The resulting object can be configured again; undo/redo restores the original
stream or the multitap and its rhythm.
